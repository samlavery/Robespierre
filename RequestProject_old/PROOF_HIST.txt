Core argument:
	1.	Starting point: the two-kernel insight
The decisive move was replacing a single cosh kernel by a paired construction with anchors at
a=\pi/6,\qquad 1-a=1-\pi/6.
Define
K_L(s,t)=\cosh((s-a)t),\qquad K_R(s,t)=\cosh((s-(1-a))t).
Then the pair sum collapses to a centered kernel:
K_L(s,t)+K_R(s,t)
=2\cosh\!\big((s-\tfrac12)t\big)\cosh\!\big((\tfrac12-a)t\big).
This is the structural heart of the approach. The paired kernel is not just “two observations”; it is a transport device that forces everything into the classical center 1/2.
	2.	Transport to the classical theta-side kernel
Let
c:=\tfrac12-\tfrac{\pi}{6}.
If the pair-side density is \psi_{\text{pair}}(t), and you define
\psi_{\text{classical}}(t):=\cosh(ct)\,\psi_{\text{pair}}(t),
then the pair integral becomes
I_{\text{pair}}(s)
=
\int_0^\infty \big(K_L(s,t)+K_R(s,t)\big)\psi_{\text{pair}}(t)\,dt
=
\int_0^\infty 2\cosh((s-\tfrac12)t)\psi_{\text{classical}}(t)\,dt.
So the two-kernel construction transports exactly to the standard centered \xi-style cosh kernel at 1/2.
	3.	Centered excess
For s=\beta+i\gamma, define the centered theta/pair excess
\Delta_\theta(\beta,\gamma)
:=
I_\theta(\beta+i\gamma)-I_\theta(\tfrac12+i\gamma).
Since
I_\theta(s)=\int_0^\infty 2\cosh((s-\tfrac12)t)\psi(t)\,dt,
write \delta=\beta-\tfrac12. Then
\Delta_\theta(\beta,\gamma)
=
2\int_0^\infty
\left(\cosh((\delta+i\gamma)t)-\cosh(i\gamma t)\right)\psi(t)\,dt.
Using
\cosh((\delta+i\gamma)t)
=
\cosh(\delta t)\cos(\gamma t)
+i\sinh(\delta t)\sin(\gamma t),
and
\cosh(i\gamma t)=\cos(\gamma t),
we get the exact decomposition
\Delta_\theta(\beta,\gamma)
=
2\int_0^\infty
\Big[(\cosh(\delta t)-1)\cos(\gamma t)
+ i\,\sinh(\delta t)\sin(\gamma t)\Big]\psi(t)\,dt.
This is the clean split into:
	•	even/cosine defect:
(\cosh(\delta t)-1)\cos(\gamma t),
	•	odd/sine defect:
i\,\sinh(\delta t)\sin(\gamma t).
	4.	Quadratic energy defect
Define
C_\psi(\beta,\gamma)
:=
\int_0^\infty (\cosh((\beta-\tfrac12)t)-1)\cos(\gamma t)\psi(t)\,dt,
S_\psi(\beta,\gamma)
:=
\int_0^\infty \sinh((\beta-\tfrac12)t)\sin(\gamma t)\psi(t)\,dt.
Then
\Delta_\theta(\beta,\gamma)=2C_\psi(\beta,\gamma)+2iS_\psi(\beta,\gamma),
and therefore the energy defect is
\mathcal E_\psi(\beta,\gamma):=|\Delta_\theta(\beta,\gamma)|^2
=4C_\psi(\beta,\gamma)^2+4S_\psi(\beta,\gamma)^2.
So \mathcal E_\psi\ge 0 is immediate.
	5.	On-line vanishing and off-line detectability
At \beta=\tfrac12, \delta=0, so
\cosh(0)-1=0,\qquad \sinh(0)=0.
Hence
\Delta_\theta(\tfrac12,\gamma)=0,\qquad \mathcal E_\psi(\tfrac12,\gamma)=0.
Off-line, \delta\neq 0, and the paired/theta observable is numerically nonzero immediately.
	6.	Parseval route for averaged positivity
The intended averaged theorem is
\overline{\mathcal E}(\beta)
:=
\int_0^\infty \mathcal E_\psi(\beta,\gamma)\,d\gamma.
With
f_\delta(t):=(\cosh(\delta t)-1)\psi(t),\qquad
g_\delta(t):=\sinh(\delta t)\psi(t),
half-line cosine/sine Parseval gives
\int_0^\infty C_\delta(\gamma)^2\,d\gamma
=
\frac{\pi}{2}\int_0^\infty f_\delta(t)^2\,dt,
\int_0^\infty S_\delta(\gamma)^2\,d\gamma
=
\frac{\pi}{2}\int_0^\infty g_\delta(t)^2\,dt.
Therefore
\overline{\mathcal E}(\beta)
=
2\pi \int_0^\infty
\Big((\cosh(\delta t)-1)^2+\sinh(\delta t)^2\Big)\psi(t)^2\,dt.
This gives:
	•	if \beta=\tfrac12, then \overline{\mathcal E}(\beta)=0,
	•	if \beta\neq\tfrac12 and \psi\not\equiv 0, then
\overline{\mathcal E}(\beta)>0.
So the detector is a perfect classifier by \beta:
\overline{\mathcal E}(\beta)=0 \iff \beta=\tfrac12.
	7.	What this does and does not prove
This proves the classifier side:
\beta=\tfrac12 \Rightarrow \text{balanced},\qquad
\beta\neq\tfrac12 \Rightarrow \text{detected}.
What it does not yet prove is the bridge:
\rho\in ZD.\mathrm{NontrivialZeros}
\Rightarrow
\overline{\mathcal E}(\rho.re)=0.
That bridge is the missing piece. If you had it, RH follows immediately.
	8.	Theta modular decomposition attempt
We checked the theta modular relation numerically. Writing the density in u-coordinates, we found
\psi(-u)=\psi(u)+\sinh(u/2)
to machine precision.
So the centered theta excess decomposes into an even part minus a correction term.
What we found numerically:
	•	the centered residual is very small,
	•	the even part and correction are both large,
	•	they nearly cancel.
Example at \beta=0.55,\ \gamma\approx 14.13:
	•	centered residual \approx 1.78\times 10^{-5},
	•	even part \approx 7.92\times 10^{-1},
	•	correction \approx 7.92\times 10^{-1}.
So theta modularity gives a structured decomposition, but not automatic vanishing.
	9.	Explicit formula experiments
We tried several candidate bridges numerically.
(a) Single-pair explicit proxy
	•	moderate magnitude correlation with theta excess,
	•	not exact.
(b) Many-zero naive sum
	•	no convergence with N,
	•	wrong normalization.
(c) Exact centered quartet formula
\Delta^{\mathrm{EF}}_{\beta,\gamma}(u)
=
-2\Bigg[
e^{\beta u}\frac{\beta\cos(\gamma u)+\gamma\sin(\gamma u)}{\beta^2+\gamma^2}
+
e^{(1-\beta)u}\frac{(1-\beta)\cos(\gamma u)+\gamma\sin(\gamma u)}{(1-\beta)^2+\gamma^2}
-
2e^{u/2}\frac{\frac12\cos(\gamma u)+\gamma\sin(\gamma u)}{\frac14+\gamma^2}
\Bigg].
Numerically this failed badly as a direct match:
	•	moderate magnitude correlation \sim 0.868,
	•	wrong complex direction,
	•	essentially purely real while theta excess was mostly imaginary.
	10.	The rotation discovery
When we allowed a complex rotation of the quartet proxy, the best-fit coefficient was almost purely imaginary:
	•	for \beta=0.501: phase \approx 89.999^\circ,
	•	for \beta=0.51: phase \approx 89.994^\circ,
	•	for \beta=0.55: phase \approx 89.969^\circ.
So the quartet was not “wrong,” it was in the wrong frame. That pointed to a hidden i-rotation in the centered theta construction.
	11.	First-order theorem at the critical line
This became the decisive local result.
Differentiate
\Delta_\theta(\beta,\gamma)
=
2\int_0^\infty
\Big[(\cosh((\beta-\tfrac12)t)-1)\cos(\gamma t)
+ i\,\sinh((\beta-\tfrac12)t)\sin(\gamma t)\Big]\psi(t)\,dt
in \beta and evaluate at \beta=\tfrac12.
Since
\frac{d}{d\beta}(\cosh((\beta-\tfrac12)t)-1)\Big|_{\beta=1/2}
= t\sinh(0)=0,
\frac{d}{d\beta}\sinh((\beta-\tfrac12)t)\Big|_{\beta=1/2}
= t\cosh(0)=t,
we get
\partial_\beta\Delta_\theta(1/2,\gamma)
=
2i\int_0^\infty t\sin(\gamma t)\psi(t)\,dt.
This explains the 90^\circ phase exactly:
first-order motion off the line is purely imaginary.
	12.	Numerical verification of the first-order theorem
We checked the derivative numerically by finite differences.
For example:
	•	\gamma=5: relative error \approx 2.5\times 10^{-9},
	•	\gamma=10: \approx 2.6\times 10^{-10},
	•	\gamma\approx 14.1347: \approx 1.39\times 10^{-9},
	•	\gamma=40: \approx 3.1\times 10^{-10}.
Phase was exactly 90^\circ in every case.
	13.	Odd Fourier profile identity
This is the cleanest exact transform identity we found.
Define the odd extension
g_\psi(t)=2t\,\psi(|t|).
Its full Fourier transform is
\widehat g_\psi(\gamma)
=
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-i\gamma t}g_\psi(t)\,dt.
Because g_\psi is odd,
\widehat g_\psi(\gamma)
=
-4i\int_0^\infty t\psi(t)\sin(\gamma t)\,dt.
Comparing with the derivative formula gives
\partial_\beta\Delta_\theta(1/2,\gamma)
=
-\tfrac12\,\widehat g_\psi(\gamma).
We checked this on the full line directly in Python and got machine-precision agreement:
	•	mean relative error \approx 3.39\times 10^{-13},
	•	max relative error \approx 3.76\times 10^{-13}.
This is the first exact bridge object that feels truly rigid:
the first-order centered theta defect is the odd Fourier profile of a concrete test function.
	14.	What failed after that
We tried comparing this first-order theta object to:
	•	the first-order derivative of the quartet explicit term: essentially zero, so wrong,
	•	smoothed zero kernels: poor fit,
	•	\xi'(1/2+i\gamma): perfect phase match, but terrible amplitude behavior.
In particular,
W(\gamma):=\frac{\partial_\beta\Delta_\theta(1/2,\gamma)}{\xi'(1/2+i\gamma)}
grew roughly exponentially in \gamma, so the bridge is not a simple pointwise multiple of \xi'.
	15.	Prime-side scale checks
We compared defect amplitudes against Chebyshev error \psi(x)-x.
For
D_\beta(x)=x^\beta+x^{1-\beta}-2\sqrt{x},
we found:
At x=10^{12}:
	•	\beta=0.501: D_\beta\approx 7.64\times 10^2,
	•	\beta=0.51: D_\beta\approx 7.68\times 10^4,
	•	\beta=0.55: D_\beta\approx 2.23\times 10^6.
At x=10^{18}:
	•	\beta=0.501: D_\beta\approx 1.72\times 10^6,
	•	\beta=0.51: D_\beta\approx 1.74\times 10^8,
	•	\beta=0.55: D_\beta\approx 6.07\times 10^9.
So off-line real parts would materially distort prime-error terms.
	16.	Explicit-formula prime prediction test

On a dense grid 10^2 to 10^6:

	•	replacing just the first zero pair by an off-line pair degrades the fit:
For \beta=0.501:

For \beta=0.51:

So off-line perturbations do measurably worsen prime-error prediction in the explicit-formula model.
